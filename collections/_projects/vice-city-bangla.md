GTA Vice City Bangla (VCB) is a fan-made Bengali-language modification of Grand Theft Auto:
Vice City, a video game popular worldwide in the early 2000s. Not available officially and too
expensive to import for the then Bengali market, the game attracted a vibrant fanbase of gamers
and developers, who actively pirated, patched, and played the game, usually on shared computers
in cyber cafes. As such, the game represents an often overlooked and ephemeral archive
of software and practices, rapidly receding in cultural memory. Today, under a different
technological regime, it lives in Youtube “let’s play videos” and nostalgic reminiscences
on social media.


In this paper, we connect a digital ethnography of Bengali VCB fan communities circa 2002 with
critical code studies. To understand the game as a localized cultural phenomenon, we argue,
it isn’t enough to play it. Nor is it possible to understand the source code in isolation
from its production and reception practices. What emerges finally is a comprehensive media
archeology of gaming. More than mere piracy, game mods represent a vital mode of grassroots
cultural production, in which fans proactively adapt corporately-produced content to fit local
social and technological realities.


Abstract accepted to the Special Issue on Interactive Digital Narratives: Creativity, Theory,
and Emerging Practices, *Digital Creativity* as of August, 2026.
